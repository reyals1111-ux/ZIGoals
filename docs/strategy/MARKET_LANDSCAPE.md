# Market landscape: the leading apps for Goals, Habits, Health and Wealth (research, 2026-10-03)

> **Research only. It decides nothing.**
> - **Pricing is NOT decided.** COST_MODEL.md once noted 4.99 as an early idea. It is not a working or chosen price, and nothing here recommends a price.
> - Prices are as listed on the date read, with currency and storefront. They change often; re-check before relying on one.
> - Privacy stances are **as each company states them** (store privacy labels and privacy policies). They are not audited claims.
> - User complaints are **aggregated themes from the small visible sample** of public store reviews. They are not representative surveys.
> - Sources were read on **2026-10-03 (UTC)** unless a line says otherwise. Anything that could not be confirmed is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

**Part of the [strategy pack](README.md).** Labels are explained there.

## How to read this
- **Selection:**
  - the apps are leaders by store presence (rating counts, install ranges) and by reputation in their category;
  - the selection is ours, not a ranking;
  - if an app was unavailable, a leading alternative replaced it, and the profile says so.
- **Price reference:** the US App Store in USD, unless a row names another storefront. Regional samples are in [MONETIZATION_OPTIONS.md](MONETIZATION_OPTIONS.md).
- **Store privacy labels.** Apple's App Privacy labels group data into "used to track you", "linked to you" and "not linked to you". Google Play has its own Data safety section. **Both are self-declared by the developer.**
- **What is not here:**
  - revenue, user numbers or market sizes, unless an official source states them;
  - market-intelligence estimates behind logins or paywalls.
- **How the complaint themes were gathered:**
  - Apple's public US review feed for each app, the 50 most recent reviews ([example feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=341232718/sortby=mostrecent/json)), with the star split shown so the skew is visible;
  - plus the few reviews visible on the Google Play listing;
  - paraphrased, with no names. A recent sample can be dominated by one bad update.
- **The App Store web listing shows at most 10 in-app purchase rows,** so these lists can be incomplete.

## Summary: ten findings
1. **The four-pillar app does not exist yet at scale.**
   - No established, well-rated product covers Goals, Habits, Health and Wealth natively.
   - The attempts found are small (68 and 11 App Store ratings, or 100K Android installs) or do-it-yourself Notion templates.
   - The platform hubs (Apple, Samsung, Google) cover Health only (§5).
2. **Four separate leaders cost USD 298.97 a year** on US list prices (MyFitnessPal, Habitify, Todoist, YNAB), or 235,97–273,97 € in the first year on the Belgian App Store (§10).
3. **The most repeated complaint is paywall creep.**
   - **Nutrition:** barcode scanning and custom goals moved behind paywalls.
   - **Planning:** repeating tasks behind Pro.
   - **Habits:** 3-habit caps.
   - **Billing and cancellation problems** come close behind (Fabulous, Any.do, Productive, Structured).
   - **In wealth apps, sync failures dominate** (§1–§4).
4. **Privacy splits the market.**
   - 7 of 9 nutrition leaders declare "data used to track you"; 5 of 7 App Store habit apps declare none.
   - Local-first products are loved at scale: Streaks, Loop, Daylio (company claim: 20M+ users), Strides.
   - **None of the budgeting leaders claims end-to-end encryption on its own pages.** Kubera states plainly that it is not end-to-end encrypted.
5. **AI is the 2025–26 race:**
   - photo and voice logging in nutrition;
   - coaches at Oura, Google, WHOOP and Monarch;
   - per-person models at Copilot.
   - **Accuracy and forced-AI backlash** are now complaint themes. All the leading coaches run in the cloud (§7).
6. **Platform integration is table stakes, and it needs native apps.**
   - Apple Health, Health Connect, home-screen widgets and watch apps are standard among the leaders, with no web access to HealthKit or Health Connect.
   - **Fitbit's Web API switches off on 2026-10-30.**
   - Google Fit's APIs are deprecated (§9).
7. **One-time pricing still wins charts:** Streaks is #1 paid in US Health & Fitness, and Things 3 is #3 paid in Productivity (2026-10-03).
8. **Switching tools exist in planning and money, not in nutrition:**
   - TickTick imports five rival planners, and YNAB has a Mint migration;
   - no nutrition leader documents an import from a rival;
   - MyFitnessPal's own export may be Premium-only (**UNVERIFIED**).
9. **Availability is fragmented.**
   - Monarch, Copilot and Rocket Money are on the US App Store only, and Emma is in the UK, US and Canada only.
   - On the Belgian App Store, the wealth options found were YNAB, Finary, getquin, CoinStats, Zerion, CoinTracker, Empower (US-focused) and Delta (sold by a Belgian company).
10. **Social can be private:**
    - share scores, not data (Oura Circles);
    - private by default (Strava hides the ends of routes);
    - a private profile switches social off (Duolingo).
    - Google *removed* social groups and messaging when Fitbit became Google Health (§8).

### Profiles
**MyFitnessPal**
- **Core:**
  - a food, calorie and macro diary on a very large database (company claim: 20.5M+ foods);
  - exercise, steps, water and weight;
  - an AI "Coach";
  - GLP-1 medication logging;
  - barcode, photo (Meal Scan) and voice logging (Premium);
  - meal planner, recipes and groceries (Premium+);
  - friends and forums.
- **Strengths:**
  - the largest footprint in the set;
  - phone, watch and web;
  - a long device list (company claim: 35+ apps and devices).
- **Privacy, as stated:**
  - **App Store label:** identifiers and advertising data are used to track; health and fitness data is linked to the person.
  - **Play Data safety:** IDs and precise location are shared with third parties ([Data safety](https://play.google.com/store/apps/datasafety?id=com.myfitnesspal.android&hl=en&gl=US)).
  - **Privacy policy:**
    - it describes targeted advertising and cross-device tracking with ad partners;
    - it says data from HealthKit, Health Connect and Samsung Health is not used for advertising ([privacy policy](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/privacy-policy)).
- **Complaints** (sample 14×1★, 6×2★, 6×3★, 1×4★, 23×5★):
  - formerly free features behind the paywall, and frequent upsell prompts;
  - a redesign that slows logging;
  - intrusive ads in the free tier;
  - freezes and goal resets;
  - AI features seen as low value by some.
- **Export:** the official help page was bot-walled. A search summary says CSV export is for Premium only: **UNVERIFIED**. No import path was found.

**Lose It!**
- **Core:**

  - GLP-1 support;
  - AI voice and photo logging, and the barcode scanner (Premium);
  - free friends and groups (company claims: 57 million users helped; 56M+ foods).
- **Strengths:**
  - every Apple device class, including Mac and Vision;

- **Privacy, as stated:**
  - **App Store label:** email, IDs and usage are used to track; sensitive info is linked.
  - **Play Data safety:** user and device IDs are shared ([Data safety](https://play.google.com/store/apps/datasafety?id=com.fitnow.loseit&hl=en&gl=US)).
  - **Privacy policy:** loseit.com could not be reached, so its policy claims are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Complaints** (13×1★, 2×2★, 5×3★, 3×4★, 27×5★):
  - free features moved to Premium (the iOS barcode scanner is cited most), and price rises;
  - upsells and ads, even for payers;

  - lag.

**What the nutrition leaders have in common:**

- **most free tiers carry ads and tracking;** MacroFactor and FatSecret are the clear exceptions on tracking;
- **paywall creep** (features that were free becoming paid) is the most repeated complaint across the set.

**Store rules that matter for health features:**
- **Apple App Store Review Guidelines** ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), page dated 2026-06-08), paraphrased:
  - 1.4.1: closer scrutiny for apps that could give inaccurate health data; remind people to consult a doctor;
  - 4.2: an app must be more than a repackaged website;
  - 5.1.1(v): in-app account deletion when accounts exist;
  - 5.1.2(vi) and 5.1.3:
    - no use of health data for advertising, marketing or data mining;
    - no false data written into HealthKit;
    - no personal health information stored in iCloud.
- **The HealthKit documentation** limits use to health and fitness purposes, and bars selling to ad platforms or data brokers ([protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)).
- **Google Play:**
  - since April 2024, **every** app on Play files a Health apps declaration, even one without health features ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291?hl=en));
  - health apps need an in-app privacy policy link and a "consult a professional" reminder ([Health Content and Services](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en));
  - the Health Connect policy lists, among prohibited uses, use "with apps that sync data between incompatible devices or platforms". The scope is undefined on the page, so a cross-platform end-to-end encrypted sync deserves a legal read before it carries Health Connect data ([Health Connect policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241?hl=en)). Flag: LC§2, LC§5.

**What this means for ZIGoals.** In-app Health already promises "Apple Health, Health Connect, Fitbit & Garmin" as planned.
- **Apple Health and Health Connect** need native apps.
- **Fitbit's Web API is being switched off,** so a Fitbit link would go through the Google Health API and its restricted-scope review.
- **Garmin** needs a business application.
- **The cloud APIs** (Google Health, Garmin, Oura, Withings) all need a server for OAuth and webhooks. That server would hold access tokens and receive readable health data before the device encrypts it: a ⚠ **private** design question.
