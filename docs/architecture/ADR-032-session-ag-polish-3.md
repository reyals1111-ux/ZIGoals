## Decisions taken without asking

- **L2 — Governance's switch does not enter the synced pages format (Part 8).** The settings v3 `pages` record is a strict object whose `items` are `z.partialRecord(z.enum([...PAGE_IDS, ...BUTTON_IDS]))`.
  - Measured with this lockfile's zod: a record with an id the reader does not know fails with `unrecognized_keys`. So a `governance` id written there would make builds #37–#47 refuse the whole settings v3 record, which breaks a rollback.
  - Instead, Governance's choice is `governanceShown?: boolean` in the device-only Earn preferences, `zigoals:earn-prefs:v1`. That record is a loose object since Session AF: older builds keep the field and ignore it.
  - This is labelled TIER 3 (stored preference), proven with the frozen readers.
- **L3 — The phone's Path/List switch stays in ⋯ (Part 3).** At 360 px the top bar's 336 px would need about 400 px for X, the progress words, the streak, "+", "⋯" and a two-button switch, all at 44 px. The switch does not fit, so it stays in the ⋯ menu on phones, as the brief allows. Tablets and computers keep it in the bar.
- **L4 — The phone's line is a river through each node (owner Part 1.1, 1.3).** *Why the phone failed twice:* AG Polish 2's ribbon (ADR-031 K16) still obeyed reference 01. Each stretch left a ring tangentially at one point and entered the next at another, heading the same way at both, so the line hugged each ring top-in/bottom-out and could not swing out into the free zones.
  - *Now* (`riverLine`/`riverSegments` in the new `lib/path/phone-river.ts`, used by FlowLine on phones): the line flows THROUGH each node's centre, drawn under it. It meets the ring wherever the flow crosses it, and its visible ends are cut exactly at the rings (de Casteljau).
  - Each stretch is one cubic. At each node the river keeps one direction. The first choice is the way its neighbours lie, turned by a seeded meander (20–38°) whose side alternates node to node, so the stretches bow one way and then the other like the computer's line (reference 05).
  - A stretch that breaks a rule tries its two nodes' other directions (gentler meanders, the plain course, ±18°/±34°, straight down) and handle lengths, two rounds at most. Never a loop, never `Math.random`.
  - The hard rules:
    - the control points keep going down, so the line never climbs and never crosses itself;
    - no radius under 40 px × width / 390 (+1 px for sampling);
    - inside the column;
    - 6 px clear of every other ring and planet, every name tab (at its reserved one-line width) and every banner's words plus their 44 px tint;
    - the turn hidden under a node is at most 30° each side, so at most 60° in all.
  - A tab may never have the line leave toward it by more than 12°.
  - Planets keep top-in, bottom-out (vertical at the top and bottom rim, as before). The finale keeps its upright side pass.
  - Soft: the line rather passes beside a NEXT badge than under it; the badge is always drawn above the line.
  - Removed: `ribbonSegments`, `passOf`, `curveFacts`, the `ribbon`/`obstacles`/`minRadius` options of `flowSegments`, and `lib/path/phone-lanes.ts`.
- **L5 — The phone's layout is the river's (owner Part 1.2, 1.4).** `phoneRiver` (same file) places each node, the side its tab hangs and the space above it, seeded from the path's id.
  - *Its constants* are the phone's CSS as measured in Chromium at 320–430 px:
    - the stage is the screen less 24 px;
    - a banner is its planet's diameter + 10, and its steps start 8 px below it;
    - the planet stands 4 px from the right edge;
    - a tab starts 12.4 px below its node's centre and 18.6 px beside it, and is 28.5 px tall.
  - *x:* each orbit flows out of its planet in a leftward sweep: a short one that stays in the right third, or a wide one across. Then come runs of short steps (12–22 % of the width) and wide sweeps (30–46 %).
    - A run turns at an edge (then sweeps wide back across), and now and then sooner, but only once it has reached that side's outer third.
    - A run toward an outer third not reached yet sweeps wide more often. A path of four or more nodes that has missed one goes there before its end.
    - The path's first three nodes take short steps, so the first screen holds three or four.
    - An orbit's last node stands 32–54 % of the width left of the next planet.
  - *Tabs:* each hangs on the side the line comes from, above it: the computer's own rule, in on the name's side and out on the other.
    - A name too long for that side hangs on the other where it fits (not before a planet), and the line leaves downward under it. Otherwise the node moves on along its run until the whole name fits on one line.
    - Only a name too long even at the edge wraps there (K5's rule), every line shown, never "…".
  - *Spaces:* each orbit goes through a palette in a seeded order (42, 58, 74, 98 px from a node's foot to the next one's top, plus 12 % of the sideways travel), so any four in a row hold a close one and a far one.
    - The model's own line is then drawn. Each stretch that breaks a rule widens its space by 16 px plus 6 % of its sideways travel, up to 22 rounds and 360 px per space.
    - Where one still breaks a rule, its lower node is pulled a third of the way back toward the station above, and the layout runs again (twice at most).
    - In an orbit of five or more whose spaces came out within 32 px of each other, one later space opens by 36 (or 52) px, kept only if the line still keeps every rule.
    - On a phone's first screen at most four nodes: the fifth moves down past it by at most 80 px.
  - *The page follows the model:*
    - each step's `--lane-x` and `--lane-gap` (its margin), each section's `--orbit-gap` and the stage's `--finale-gap`, phones only (≤ 640 px, `[data-lanes]`; amendment 3);
    - the line is drawn by the same `riverSegments` from the measured page;
    - the river depends on the path's shape (orbits, ids, names, planets), the width and the first screen's foot only, never on a tick.
  - *Cost:* about 50–100 ms for 13 nodes and up to ~300 ms for 30 in this sandbox, once per layout; stretches are cached by their shape.

- **L10 — River v2 (owner amendment 6).** `lib/path/phone-river.ts` is rewritten. L4–L6 still describe the frame (through each node's centre, cut at the rings, planets top-in/bottom-out, the page following the model, no jump) but not the shape. In the shape:
  - *Each stretch is the computer's own:* two cubics joined at a seeded waypoint with one tangent there. The tangent is the swing, from where the line heads out of the upper station to where it heads into the lower one.
    - The waypoint's place across (40–60 %), its turning height (42–58 %) and its sideways offset are seeded per stretch.
    - The offset's side alternates from stretch to stretch, now and then (1 in 5) staying the same.
    - The offset is 8–16 % of the column, or as much as a stretch whose ends lie closer needs to swing 30 % of the column.
    - Tried in order: that waypoint, the other side, wider either side, half. Handles are 42 % then 58 % of each piece.
  - *At each node* the river leans 8–20° away from the node's tab (toward the side it swings on to); then upright, twice the lean, and a little the other way. It never leans more than 12° toward the tab.
  - *Hard rules, added to L4's:* a visible swing of ≥ 30 % of the column + 2 px, and a bow of ≥ 5 % of the column + 1 px beside its own chord.
  - *Nodes* stand by zone: the left third, the right third, or the middle. Zones are chosen as the cheapest sequence that keeps rule 2 (an exact dynamic programme over the last two stations). The costs:
    - a swing across: 0;
    - into or out of the middle: 1.5;
    - the same zone twice: 6, or 12 among the first three;
    - the middle among the first three: +6. The middle is used only where the name fits there on one line, and never beside a planet;
    - a node in the right third beside a planet: 50;
    - plus a seeded nudge of up to 1 per choice.
  - *Positions:* an orbit's first node, out of its planet, stands toward the outer edge of the left third, so the planet's sweep spans the width. Other nodes stand in the inner 70 % of their third, where a swing across costs the least height.
  - *Tabs:* each hangs on the side the line comes from. A long name moves its node within its zone toward the edge that gives room; else its tab hangs on the other side; else it wraps there. The node keeps its zone.
  - *Spaces:* a stretch's starting space is at least 55 % of its sideways travel less 20 px. The first three nodes start from the close half of the palette.
    - Where the third node just misses the first screen, the first three spaces give back up to 60 px, 6 px at a time, while the line keeps every rule.
    - The spread step opens the widest space (or closes the closest), so the spread grows.
  - *Measured over 960 layouts* (240 paths × 4 widths):
    - mean node-to-node pitch 180 px;
    - first screen: 3.0 whole nodes on average at both 390 and 360. Every qualifying path shows at least 3 at 390; at 360, 2 of the qualifying paths show 2;
    - about 95 ms a layout, at most about 0.3 s, in this sandbox.
- **L11 — How the owner's rules are read and checked (owner amendment 6).**
  - *The rules* live in `lib/path/river-rules.test-helper.ts`, which reads only the drawn path data and the stations' centres:
    - **Rule 1, per stretch:** two cubics with no corner (≤ 1°) at the waypoint; an excursion (max x − min x of the visible curve) of ≥ 30 % of the column; a bow beside its own chord of ≥ 5 % of the column.
      - Between two nodes, the swings (the way each stretch travels across) never go the same way three times in a row, and at least half of the neighbouring pairs turn the other way.
    - **Rule 2, per window**, over the river's stations: the nodes and each orbit's planet, which stands in the right third beside its banner, so a planet counts as a right-third station. The planet must have the line top-in/bottom-out, so an orbit's first and last nodes stand to its left.
      - Within every three stations in a row: never all in one half; the left third and the right third both present.
    - **Rule 3, the first screen** (from the stage's top under the header to the timer row): the line spans ≥ 65 % of the width; it reverses its sideways way at least twice (a reversal counts after 12 px of travel); stations stand on both halves.
      - Checked for paths whose first orbit has three nodes or more. A first orbit of one or two hands the screen to the next banner.

- **L13 — River v3 (owner amendment 7): the computer's own line, a seeded walk for the nodes.**
  - *The line is the literal port.* A phone draws `flowSegments` itself, unchanged, through the stations `lib/path/phone-river.ts` places: in on the name's side, out on the other, at side points heading straight down; same side → a bow out past both rims, across → a swing through the middle; seeded amplitude, direction and turning height per stretch (from the two ids). Swing: `phoneSwing(w) = min(150, 0.4 × w)` (146 at 390). The unit test checks that the model's line equals `flowSegments` with the phone's swing.
  - *What the phone changed, and why (the smallest pieces):*
    - **Node placement.** The computer's 8-step wave (0, ±0.5, ±1 × swing) moves each node half a swing: in a 366 px column that is five fixed places, and no neighbour ever more than 73 px across, so no sweep is possible. That would fail the variety rules by construction, so the phone walks instead: for each node, a seeded grid of places (15 across the column, both name sides), the stretch to each drawn by `flowSegments` and classified by its own shape; the plan's kind first, else the next. Every run of five holds each kind (the bow twice); the path's first run puts a drift, a sweep and a bow first.
    - **The finale** takes the line in at its top on a phone (it stands in the column's middle under the river); the computer's finale keeps its side entry.
    - **No lead-in and no tail on a phone.** The first planet opens the stage (its top is 7 px down), and the tail in the 70 px under the finale would turn tighter than the least radius for every seeded amplitude (measured on all 240 paths).
    - **Heights.** A stretch that breaks a rule (radius, column, clearances) gets more height, 8 px at a time, within its kind's range (+60 px; +110 for a bow); the place that keeps every rule with the least height wins (a bow: the nearest place below, so it reads as a loop).
    - **Names.** On a phone most names only fit hanging toward the middle, so the line, which leaves on the other side, leaves toward the near edge; a node keeps room on its way out for its next stretch's seeded bow (known from the two ids). One step of backtracking per node in an orbit (at most four an orbit) where the next node finds no place; a long name that fits only far across gets the height its loop needs (up to 460 px, rare).
    - **Seed.** The checklist's path id is the constant `checklist-today`, so the walk is seeded by the path id and its own node ids: every path its own walk, the same on every visit; a tick changes no id.
    - **First screen.** Its three nodes start from their kind's least height; where the third still misses, the first spaces give back what the rules allow, then the walk runs again "compact" (the place of any kind needing the least height). Where the first two screens lack a drift, a sweep or a bow, the next of the six orders of the first run is tried (the first that shows all three, else the best).
    - **Spread.** In every orbit of four nodes or more one space opens (or closes) just enough that the spaces spread 62 px, kept only while every stretch keeps the rules and the first screen keeps its three nodes.
  - *Kinds, by shape* (`stretchKind`; the test helper has its own copy): a **bow** when both ends are on the same side of their nodes, the line passes 16 px or more beyond both rims there and the two nodes stand within 30 % of the width of each other; else a **sweep** when it travels 45 % of the width or more; else a **swing** when it crosses the middle; else a **drift**.
  - *Measured* over the 240 seeded paths of the unit test (names 3–40 characters, 320–430 px): drift 44 %, sweep 21 %, bow 20 %, swing 15 % of 4,761 stretches; the fullest 20 % band holds 39 % of the nodes; centre-to-centre spacing p10 126 px, median 169, p90 256; no stretch breaks a hard rule. First screen: 3–4 whole nodes at 390 on every qualifying path; at 360, 3 on all but one in twenty (L11's 90 %). About 60 ms a layout on average here, at most about 0.75 s (a long path with first-run retries), once per open.
  - *Tests:* `lib/path/river-rules.test-helper.ts` (the hard rules and the variety rules, from the drawn path data alone); `lib/path/phone-river.test.ts` (a strict zig-zag and reference 06 fail the variety rules; the 240-path property run keeps the hard rules and the variety rules; the line is `flowSegments`; the three sample paths' first views differ); `tests/checklist-phone-ag-polish-3.spec.ts` at 390 and 360 (the hard rules on the drawn line; a drift, a sweep and a bow in the first two screens; three different first views; ZIGi clear; no jump; the timer row).
